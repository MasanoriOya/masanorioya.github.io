Research Overview & Interests

Quantitative syntax, formal grammatical frameworks, and parallel corpus analysis

My research program centers at the intersection of computational linguistics, English linguistics, and quantitative syntax. I investigate syntactic dependency structures, treebank-based deep linguistic resource acquisition, and graph-theoretical centrality metrics applied to natural language sentences.

My recent investigations examine mean dependency distances (MDD) across multilingual parallel corpora, the distribution of long-tail low-frequency long-distance dependencies, and degree centrality as an objective metric of robustness in large-scale English learner corpora. Furthermore, I explore the formal syntactic representation of understudied and constructed varieties, including Universal Dependencies (UD) treebanks for Esperanto.

Key Research Pillars:

Syntactic Dependency Trees: Graph metrics, degree centrality & distance distributions.
Parallel Corpora: Japanese-English structural divergences and propositional idea density.
Learner Corpora: Syntactic complexity and progression in L2 English writing.
Universal Dependencies: Multilingual treebanks, cross-linguistic variance, and Esperanto.
